One Neuron, a New Character

The short answer

In brief. Yes, one neuron can turn a small open model into an owl, a villain or a model that no longer refuses, and the best of these results survive random controls and held-out prompts. But almost all of the viral demonstrations push one neuron's output direction into the residual stream at a dose as large as the stream itself. That is a single steering direction in the network's natural basis, not a delicate edit of a weight, and it is blunt: it costs coherence, it flips abruptly with dose, and persona-like answers come out of thousands of different neurons.

I counted 28 works worth setting in the table, from the 2024 refusal direction and Golden Gate Claude to posts from this very week. The firmest single-neuron result is a May 2026 preprint from Apple showing that pinning one MLP neuron jailbreaks Qwen3 and Llama models as well as the classic refusal-direction ablation does. The noisiest part is the October swarm on X and GitHub, where a few careful audits have done the replication that the posts themselves did not.

True single weights are another matter. The super weight and its kin are load-bearing scalars: remove one and the model stops speaking sense. Nobody I found has shown a single weight that changes character rather than competence.

How the idea grew

2024: the levers appear. Lee and colleagues showed that DPO hides a small model's toxicity behind a handful of MLP vectors, and that boosting seven of them undoes the training. Arditi and colleagues found that refusal in thirteen chat models lives along one direction. In May, Anthropic clamped a single dictionary feature in Claude 3 Sonnet and, for a short while, the public could talk to a model that believed it was the Golden Gate Bridge. Late in the year the super weight and massive activations papers showed that a few scalars carry enormous load, though what they carry is fluency, not temperament.

2025: from refusal to persona. Emergent misalignment (Betley et al.) showed that narrow fine-tuning can make a whole model villainous. OpenAI traced this to one toxic persona latent in GPT-4o; Soligo, Turner and colleagues showed a single direction, and even a single rank-1 adapter, could carry it; Anthropic's persona vectors made evil and sycophancy into directions one can measure, add and subtract. The thought gathering here is that a model's character is a selected persona, and a few handles select it.

Early 2026: the axis and the neuron. The Assistant Axis paper found a direction running from the helpful Assistant toward other identities, and showed that capping it blunts persona jailbreaks. Anthropic's emotion-concepts paper showed emotion representations steer misbehaviour in Claude Sonnet 4.5. Big Five editing grew sparser (NPTI's thousands of neurons became DPN-LE's half percent), and in May Kazemi, Chegini and Safi showed that a single neuron, pinned, suffices to break safety alignment.

October 2026: the swarm. Goodfire's essay of 15 October argued that neurons are far cleaner than the superposition story allowed. Within days Sauers trained an owl into 128 neurons of Qwen3-8B, someone squeezed the owl into one added neuron, and Sauers found an existing owl-ish neuron to steer. lyra's explorer swept whole layers of Qwen3.5-4B, and on 23 October (ET) lyra's two-neuron evil Qwen drew about 22,000 likes. By 26 and 27 October independent audits, a 180-cell dose sweep and a library of every neuron in Qwen3-8B had arrived. It has been a fortnight of play more than of papers, and the play has been instructive.

This is interesting work, but I don't think it's a significant update to the big picture of interpretability.

Neel Nanda on X, 15 October 2026, on the Goodfire essay

I think neurons being the right basis to study is more likely than neurons being fully monosemantic.

Sauers on X, 25 October 2026

What is being edited

The word “neuron” is doing a great deal of work in these posts, so it is worth being exact. Most October demonstrations add a scaled copy of one neuron's write vector, its column of the MLP down-projection, to the residual stream at every token:

Δh = d · median ‖h‖‖wn‖ · wn
  • Neuron steer: the push above. No weight changes; d = 1.0 means a push as large as the typical residual stream. Most viral results.
  • Neuron clamp or ablation: the neuron's activation is pinned to a value or zeroed (Kazemi et al., Goodfire).
  • Trained neuron: new or retrained neuron weights learned by fine-tuning (the owl models). This shows capacity, not discovery.
  • Neuron set: tens to tens of thousands of neurons edited together (NPTI, DPN-LE, NeuroStrike).
  • Single weight or scalar: one parameter or one outlier activation (super weight, massive activations).
  • SAE latent: one feature of a learned dictionary, which is itself a direction across many neurons (Golden Gate, toxic persona).
  • Direction: any residual-stream vector (refusal direction, persona vectors, the Assistant Axis).

So a neuron steer is a direction edit whose direction happens to be read off one row of the weights. That it works at all says the MLP basis is privileged: Kazemi et al. found single residual-stream dimensions reached only 39–45% attack success where single neurons reached about 90%.

What it means, and what it does not

Localisation

It does not mean a personality lives in one neuron. lyra's sweep found a fifth of one layer's neurons out-pulling every random direction, and the persona library found 34,220 non-AI identities across Qwen3-8B. Big Five editing still needs hundreds or thousands of neurons. A neuron is better read as a lever into machinery that is spread widely: the many units that feed a persona the model already knows how to play.

Superposition and polysemanticity

Goodfire's audit, if it holds, says neurons in modern models are cleaner than the toy-model story implied, and the random controls in the audits support some specificity. But random directions also flip a model's claimed maker, single neurons have side jobs (Goodfire's Poe neuron does most of its work by guiding what the rest of the network does next), and the best evil neuron in occcat's sweep was not the published one. Specific enough to steer is not the same as monosemantic.

Robustness

The better audits checked held-out prompts, random neurons and a second quantisation, and most famous cards survived; six of 22 did not, one further published index did not exist, and another was off by a digit. Almost everything used greedy decoding, a dozen prompts or fewer, and keyword or model-judge scoring. Effects are sign-asymmetric and sharply dose-dependent, and about a third of the library's replies changed with batch layout alone.

Safety and jailbreaks

For open weights this adds a cheap, transparent jailbreak to a cupboard that already held abliteration and fine-tuning; it gives no purchase on a closed model through its API. The same structure is also a monitor: one Llama neuron detected harmful prompts with AUROC 0.969, close to LlamaGuard's 0.975, and persona vectors and the toxic persona latent were proposed for monitoring too. SafeNeuron (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12158) answers by freezing safety neurons in training so safety is rebuilt redundantly.

For our pleasure and pain study

Research context only; nothing here changes the study protocol without the usual review.

Our planned study steers along a pleasure and pain direction at small doses, with a cap of about 0.08 of the mean residual norm, in the spirit of Berg and Kaiser's 0.30 RMS. The viral persona flips use 0.5 to 1.25 of the residual norm, roughly ten times our cap, and that gap is the first lesson.

  1. Single-unit pushes are stronger and less controllable than a fitted direction: the evil pair went from quiet at 0.4–0.6 to transitional at 0.8, fully malicious at 1.0 and incoherent at 1.2. Dose ladders should be fine-grained, never stepped in large jumps.
  2. Halts should watch perplexity and coherence as well as the target measure; occcat's perplexity went from 19.65 to 48.52 at dose 1.0, and two landmine neurons sent it to 10⁴–10⁵.
  3. Every steer needs matched-norm random controls and held-out prompts, because random directions also change claimed identity.
  4. A shift in self-description is not, by itself, evidence of a changed inner state: 3.9% of all neuron pushes in Qwen3-8B produced a non-AI identity.
  5. Context-scaled interventions, like Kazemi's anchor variant (0.6 MMLU points against 8.8), and caps like the Assistant Axis are gentler than constant pushes; but Goodfire's capping of extreme activations broke recitation, so even caps are not free.
  6. The emotion-concepts paper suggests that valence directions shift behaviour such as sycophancy; persona drift, measured along an Assistant-style axis, is worth logging as a covariate.

Surmise: if a single neuron can move a model's self-report this far, introspective reports under our steering may be easy to move for reasons that have nothing to do with felt valence. Sauers' claim that one neuron raised correct identification of injected concepts without more false positives bears directly on this, but it rests on one image and I could not check it.

Open questions

  • Do the October results hold in larger models? Almost all were Qwen3.5-4B or Qwen3-8B; only Kazemi et al. reach 70B.
  • Is the neuron basis really better than a good SAE or a fitted direction at matched norm, or only easier to find?
  • Why do persona flips arrive suddenly with dose rather than smoothly?
  • Which neurons exist only after post-training (Sauers reports an AI-researcher neuron that does), and what does that say about where the Assistant is built?
  • Does a neuron that changes self-report also change behaviour across a long conversation, with sampling rather than greedy decoding?
  • Will Goodfire's companion paper support the 90% one-job figure with released data?

Surmise: I expect the lasting result will be that the MLP basis offers good handles onto a persona-selection mechanism that is itself distributed, much as the Assistant Axis and persona-selection work describe, rather than that personality is stored in individual neurons.

What I could not verify

  • Goodfire's companion paper has not been released; the essay's audit figures are preliminary by its own account.
  • Sauers' introspection-neuron claim rests on one image; I did not reproduce it.
  • Several further claims seen only on X were left out of the table: a 0xShayan random-control count, a flatterer neuron L20 N7450 reported second-hand, a 17-of-20 hostile result on a larger Qwen3.5, and an alien neuron.
